# A release ladder: what can go out without talking to a lawyer

**Written 2026-10-18 by a Claude session, because the owner asked whether a release is possible without a lawyer ("I hate talking to humans").** Not legal advice. The honest answer: **nothing legally requires hiring a lawyer to publish software**, and the steps below are chosen so the first public release needs none. A lawyer matters at the higher rungs, where strangers' words, children or employees come in; even there the contact can be one written exchange (see the end).

**Current open release actions:** ED18–ED20 and O10–O12 in `docs/open-items.md`. The task descriptions below are release guidance, not a separate backlog.

The idea: climb only as far as the risks you can cover yourself. Each rung adds duties.

| Rung | What goes out | What you must do yourself (no lawyer) | What would justify a lawyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The local edition** (free download, `docs/plan-editions.md`) | A program on the person's own computer that collects nothing and sends nothing | The five tasks below | Nothing, if the five tasks are done and nothing phones home |
| **2. Hosted, invitation only** (what exists now: circles, an account cap, 13 and over once stated) | People you or a circle leader know, on your server | Keep it small and known (`MAX_ACCOUNTS`, invitation codes); keep the privacy notice true; the 13+ line (ED14) once the local edition exists | Before it takes strangers |
| **3. A separate public forum**, invitation to join, a hired moderation team | Strangers writing to each other on a site you run | Written rules, a report route, moderator guidelines; **contracts with the moderators** (they see and remove people's words) | A one-time written review of the terms, the privacy text and the moderator contract |
| **4. Option A** (no age floor on the hosted copy) | Children's accounts on your server | Everything in `docs/plan-no-age-floor.md` section 2 | Yes: parental consent is the part not to guess at |

## The five tasks that make rung 1 safe

1. **Know where everything in the download came from.** This is the real exposure, and it is a matter of facts, not law. What is known (2026-10-18): the owner says the **tarot text, the concepts and the spreadsheets were written by Felix G and Kylie B**, so those are first-party work with **two authors**. The archetype lists in `data/` say in their headers that Claude wrote them at the owner's direction (text written by an AI may not be protected by copyright in some countries; that does not stop a release, it only means a licence may cover less than assumed). **Card pictures (owner, 2026-10-18):** the 78 pictures in `public/cards` (added 2026-04-09, "Add card images to repo") came from berkough.itch.io/tarotjs. That page gives the BSD 3-clause licence for the *code*; it says the deck is Pixie-Smith, credits Luciella Elisabeth Scarlett for cleaning scans taken from Wikipedia, and states no licence for the images. BSD 3-clause asks only that the copyright notice, the conditions and the disclaimer travel with the code, and that the author's name is not used to endorse your product; it is compatible with GPL v3. **Answer to "can we use them?": very likely yes, but not because of the BSD line.** The Pixie-Smith art dates from 1909 (the artist, Pamela Colman Smith, died in 1951), which is the basis for treating the original deck as public domain in most countries; the cleaned scans come from https://luciellaes.itch.io/rider-waite-smith-tarot-cards-cc0 (Luciella Elisabeth Scarlett; found by the owner). **What that page says (read 2026-10-18):** the CC0 dedication covers the *cardback design*; for the card illustrations she says she assessed the originals as public domain under Australian law and asks each user to assess their own case; credit is optional; please do not sell the cards as they are (an app that adds its own work is the use she describes). So the pictures are usable here on the strength of the original art being public domain, with a CC0-titled, credit-welcome pack as the source; the owner should record that they judge it public domain in the countries of release. The credits file thanks Luciella and Pamela Colman Smith. What is **not** known: there is **no `LICENSE` file**, and whether these 78 files are exactly the cleaned scans (a public-domain scan and a modern edition look the same in the repository). The encyclopedia was seeded from spreadsheets that live outside the repository (named "St. Cepesa Horat - ..."), and a local download has to ship that data to work: if that is a name the two authors wrote under, say so and the credit line will use it. For each item write one line: what it is, who made it, under what terms. **If any line says "not sure", leave that item out of the download** (the local edition can show text only, or let the person add their own pictures) and put it back when the answer is known. That removes the risk without asking anyone.
   - **Felix G is a co-author, so the licence needs his agreement.** This is the one place a person has to be asked, and a written yes is enough. A draft you can send as it is (and keep his reply as the record): "Hi Felix. I plan to release Divination Station as a free download. The tarot text, concepts and spreadsheets we wrote together would be part of it, under the [licence name] licence, which lets anyone use and share it [one line on the conditions]. Are you happy with that? A reply of yes is enough. If you would rather have a different licence, a credit line in a particular form, or some parts left out, tell me and I will do that instead."
2. **Pick a licence.** **Chosen 2026-10-18: GNU AGPL v3** (https://choosealicense.com/licenses/agpl-3.0/; the owner first named GPL v3 and switched the same day). It lets anyone use, change and share the code, and requires anyone who passes on a copy, or lets other people use a modified copy over a network (section 13), to offer those people the source under the same licence. That closes the gap GPL v3 leaves: nobody can run a closed, modified hosted copy. It does not bind the owner's own site, and it does not touch a person who only uses the app. It is written for code, so the text, data and pictures keep their own lines (task 1), and a text or data licence such as CC BY-SA can be chosen for the tarot text with Felix G. The libraries the app uses (MIT, Apache 2.0, SIL OFL fonts) are compatible with GPL v3 and so with AGPL v3 (which is GPL v3 plus the network paragraph); the FSF lists the 3-clause BSD licence and Apache 2.0 as compatible, and a `NOTICE` file should keep their credits. The `LICENSE` file is in place (AGPL v3 text from gnu.org); Felix G's yes is still owed. The original note on the choice follows. The choice is the owner's: a permissive licence (MIT) lets anyone do anything, including running a closed hosted copy; a "share-alike" licence (AGPL) requires anyone who runs a modified copy for others to publish their changes. The licence also carries the "no warranty" clause. It covers code only; the data and pictures need their own lines from task 1.
3. **Say what the app is not.** "For reflection; not medical, mental-health, legal or financial advice" is already in the app's text; copy it into the download's first screen and the README.
4. **A true privacy statement.** "This app keeps everything on your computer and sends nothing to the owner." Short, and only true if nothing phones home: no analytics, no error reporting, no update check unless the person presses a button. The sources found say a policy is not strictly required for an app that processes no personal data, that stating it is cheap and often expected by app stores, and that sources differ, so state it. If the person pastes in their own Claude key, the words they send go to Anthropic under their account: say so on the screen where they paste it.
5. **Check the name.** Search "Divination Station" in the trademark registers of the countries you care about and on the web for an existing product. Free, and it takes minutes.

## If one written exchange is ever worth it

- **Email only, a flat fee, specific questions.** Many lawyers will review a document or answer a short list in writing. Claude can prepare a **one-page packet** (what the product does, what data it holds, the three questions, the draft texts) so the whole exchange is one email out and one back.
- **Free or low-cost places to look** (found by search; **not checked that they are open to a solo developer now**): the Software Freedom Law Center (pro bono help to free-software developers, historically for projects and non-profits), the Open Source Initiative's Legal Reference Resource (it does not give advice; it helps find lawyers who know open source and lists questions to ask them), and law-school technology or IP clinics.

## What this does not cover

US state laws on minors online, the Online Safety Act and EU digital-services rules for a public forum, tax and company questions if money ever changes hands, and hiring (the moderators). Rung 1 avoids all of them by collecting nothing and hosting nothing.
